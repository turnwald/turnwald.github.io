## Research question

On what explicitly bounded domain can a learned barrier candidate satisfy or fail safety conditions, compared with an analytic baseline?

## Minimum scope

Use a self-contained point-robot simulation with a known safe set and input limits, an analytic CBF and a small neural candidate. Apply sampling, counterexample search and one agreed verification or bounding method; examine repair or retraining using found counterexamples. No robot or ROS is required.

## Evaluation

Compare analytic and learned candidates using condition violations, counterexamples, closed-loop behavior and computation. State the exact domain and assumptions. Failure to verify is a valid result; sampled success is not a proof.

## Prerequisites

Strong Python, control theory, neural networks and optimization or verification.

## Expected deliverables

Baseline and training scripts, falsification and verification experiments, visualizations, precise claim limits and thesis report.

## First Iteration

**Objective:** Explore sampled barrier-condition checks and their limits in a bounded obstacle-avoidance example.

**Task:**

Create a minimal neural CBF prototype for a bounded 2D obstacle-avoidance problem.

The first iteration may use a point robot with simple dynamics. Define a safe set, train or mock a small neural barrier candidate, and test whether the candidate satisfies the CBF condition on sampled states. Include at least one counterexample or a clear statement that none was found in the sampled domain.

The first iteration should show:

- safe, unsafe, and boundary samples,
- an analytic CBF or known safe-set baseline,
- the learned barrier visualization,
- sampled CBF-condition violations or counterexample search results,
- a short explanation of why simulation success alone is not a certificate.

**Deliverables:** Provide the reproducible prototype, a visualization of the analytic and candidate barriers and sampled outcomes, and a short limitations note.

**Evaluation criteria:** Assess the definition of the safe set and condition, the baseline comparison and sampled violations, and the distinction between numerical testing and proof.
