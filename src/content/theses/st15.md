## Research question

How do planning methods compare in path quality and executed-trajectory robustness on reproducible planetary-rover tasks?

## Minimum scope

Build a standalone benchmark using existing Gazebo rover/world assets, common start-goal cases and two classical planners. Include a simple tracker and at least one controlled tracking or terrain disturbance. An existing baseline may be reused.

## Evaluation

Compare planned and executed paths using success, clearance, terrain-risk exposure, path length, tracking error and computation time over repeated cases. A bachelor scope may use a smaller scenario set.

## Prerequisites

Python or C++, Gazebo/ROS 2, planning and basic robotics.

## Expected deliverables

Benchmark tasks, planner and tracker interfaces, seeded runs, logs, metrics, plots, documentation and thesis report.

## First Iteration

**Objective:** Compare planning methods quantitatively on small rover-like path-planning scenarios.

**Task:**

Create a minimal trajectory or path-planning benchmark for a rover-like robot.

The first iteration may use a simple 2D grid or height-map approximation instead of the full Gazebo planetary environment. Define at least two start-goal problems and compare at least two planning methods, for example A*, RRT/RRT*, spline smoothing, or another classical planner. The comparison should use quantitative metrics.

The first iteration should show:
- two start-goal scenarios,
- two different planned paths or trajectories,
- metrics such as path length, smoothness, computation time, obstacle clearance, or terrain-risk exposure,
- a short explanation of how the benchmark could later be moved into lunar or Martian Gazebo worlds.

**Deliverables:** Provide the code, plots of the paths for both scenarios, a compact metrics table and a short explanation of possible transfer to simulation.

**Evaluation criteria:** Assess reproducibility, a consistent comparison of methods and scenarios, and whether the reported metrics are meaningful.
