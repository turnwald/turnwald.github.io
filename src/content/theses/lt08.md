## Research question

How do a small set of disturbances affect the reliability of a navigation–pick–place task, and how does one defined mitigation affect the outcome?

## Minimum scope

Use ROS 2 and Gazebo to create one reproducible navigation–pick–place scenario in a simplified clinical environment, with a mobile base, manipulator, gripper and an object with contact interactions. Predefined navigation goals and simple scripted grasping are sufficient; advanced perception and grasp planning are not mandatory. Evaluate a small, defined set of disturbances, such as base-position and object-pose errors, and compare the nominal approach with one clearly defined mitigation. Deformable packaging and robotic peeling are outside scope. Use an existing representative model or a simplified stand-in if the relevant robot assets are unavailable, and document the differences. Completion must not depend on other projects, hardware or the availability of a specific robot model.

## Evaluation

Use the same repeated seeded cases to compare the nominal approach and the chosen mitigation. Report task success, failure modes, collisions or undesired contacts, completion time and sensitivity to each disturbance. Document simulation assumptions and the limits of any conclusions about a physical robot.

## Prerequisites

ROS 2, Gazebo, Python or C++, basic navigation and manipulation, and experimental design.

## Expected deliverables

Nominal scenario and launch files, disturbance configuration, baseline and mitigation, reusable logs and analysis scripts, interface documentation and thesis report.
