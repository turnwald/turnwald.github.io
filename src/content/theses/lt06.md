## Research question

How accurately and reliably can multiple calibrated cameras localize marked mobile robots, including under occlusion?

## Minimum scope

Calibrate cameras, estimate marker poses in a common frame, fuse observations, and publish poses via a documented ROS 2 interface. A representative recorded or simulated dataset may support the complete thesis; access to physical robots is not required.

## Evaluation

Measure pose error against available ground truth, update rate, latency and failures, including occlusion or camera handover. Compare single-camera and fused estimates.

## Prerequisites

Python or C++, ROS 2, Linux, camera calibration and coordinate frames.

## Expected deliverables

Reproducible localization pipeline, calibration and frame documentation, datasets or generation scripts, evaluation plots and thesis report. This is a lab infrastructure topic; a direct safety-control contribution is not required.

## First Iteration

**Objective:** Demonstrate a basic marker-localization pipeline and explain how it could support multi-camera tracking.

**Task:**

Create a minimal ArUco localization prototype before the first meeting.

The prototype may use a webcam, a recorded image sequence, or synthetic/example images. It does not need to use the final lab camera setup yet. The goal is to demonstrate marker detection, pose estimation or at least image-plane localization, and a clear output format.

The first iteration should show:

- successful detection of at least one ArUco marker,
- marker ID and marker position or pose output,
- one visualization image or short video with detected marker corners/axes,
- a short explanation of the coordinate frame and what would be required for multi-camera fusion,
- the commands or script used to run the example.

The topic will only be assigned if the student can explain the detection pipeline, the calibration requirement, and the difference between image coordinates, camera coordinates, and a global lab frame.

**Deliverables:** Provide the runnable script and commands, a detection visualization, and a short explanation of frames and calibration.

**Evaluation criteria:** Assess reproducibility, marker detection and output, correct distinction between coordinate frames, and a clear account of the requirements for fusion.
